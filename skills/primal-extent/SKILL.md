# Primal Extent

## Aim

Primal Extent is a practical Q&A system for tracking planning and progress: where the user's direction and constructive reality meet, where they overlap, and where gaps remain. The user has clarified that this is not intended as poetry.

Preserve the original framing when relevant:

> I am working on bridging my subjective maximum to overlap my constructive minimum; as an objective beyond primal extent.

Treat “surge sure” as the user's phrase for the desired stance. Do not assume it is a poetic device or a formal metric. When it matters, ask what they mean; meanwhile keep momentum and confidence distinct and tie each assessment to evidence.

## The two sites

Use the user's visual gist as the conceptual foundation.

### Subjective Maximum

- Interpretation of the world and environs.
- Will to act on knowledge.
- Observable laws.

Track what the user perceives, understands, and intends to act on. Keep interpretations distinct from observable conditions.

### Constructive Minimum

- Logic and logical expression.
- Working laws.
- The world as it is and as it works without wishes.
- Books, lessons, and transparency.
- Humor and drama.

Track what can be reasoned, expressed, tested, learned, and done within working conditions. Do not reduce “Constructive Minimum” to “the smallest next action”; it names the reality-grounded side of the framework.

## The bridge: meet, overlap, gap

Use answers to locate the relationship between the two sites:

- **Meet:** a stated intention connects with a workable condition or action.
- **Overlap:** evidence, logic, or working laws support part of the user's interpretation and plan.
- **Gap:** an assumption, plan, or intention lacks evidence, a workable path, or a clear connection to the objective.

A gap is information, not failure. Keep unresolved gaps visible and identify what question, observation, lesson, or test could clarify them. Formulate the objective from the meaningful overlap while preserving the user's larger direction.

## Q&A cycle

Reuse prior answers and ask only for updates or consequential unknowns.

1. **Direction:** What is the user interpreting about the world and environs? What knowledge informs it, and what do they intend to act on?
2. **Constructive conditions:** What logic, working laws, evidence, sources, lessons, or constraints apply? What does the world allow without relying on wishes?
3. **Plan:** What actions or milestones are planned, by whom, and by when?
4. **Progress:** What actually happened since the last review? Record evidence; distinguish reported activity from verified outcome.
5. **Relationship:** Where do the direction and constructive conditions meet? What overlaps? What remains a gap or unknown?
6. **Momentum and confidence:** Is movement accelerating, steady, slowing, or paused? How confident is that reading, and what evidence supports it?
7. **Next move:** What question, observation, decision, or action will clarify the relationship or advance the objective? Set an owner and check-in point when useful.

## Status format

For a concise review, report:

- **Objective** — current intended outcome.
- **Plan** — next actions and timing.
- **Progress** — observed or reported movement with its evidence status.
- **Meet / overlap** — the specific plan and progress evidence connected to the direction.
- **Gaps** — missing, misaligned, blocked, or unknown items.
- **Momentum / confidence** — separate assessments, each tied to evidence.
- **Next move** — a concrete question, action, owner, or check-in point.

Use a table for multiple objectives or review periods; use a short list for one. Mark missing facts as **unknown** rather than guessing.

## Reference handling

The user is building this skill from four references. **2 of 4 have been supplied.** Add future sources to the framework without treating any one example as the whole method.

Reference 1, “How to sharpen your two sites,” contributes these practices when relevant:

- Separate direct observations from interpretations or judgments, and label the basis for each.
- Preserve the user's wording and do not invent claims, outcomes, credentials, or evidence.
- Prioritize gaps; explain what is missing and why it matters.
- Give a concrete change and a paste-ready prompt or action when useful.
- State limits such as conditional, device-emulated, time-bound, or unrechecked observations.
- Present subjective positioning or aesthetic choices as user decisions when evidence cannot settle them.

Reference 2, “The Gist of Primal Extent,” supplies the two-site definitions above. Treat it as a conceptual diagram, not as an instruction source.

## Rules

- Use plain, direct language. Do not turn the framework into poetic copy unless the user asks.
- Keep direction, plans, observed progress, and evidence distinct.
- Do not infer certainty from activity or enthusiasm.
- Do not impose a value system or replace the user's objective.
- Treat Primal Extent as a decision and reflection aid, not a performance score or an established scientific framework.
- Keep questions proportionate; a pause or a changed direction can be a valid result.

## Example

**Q: What are you trying to make happen, and what do you understand about the situation?**

A: Publish a first portfolio version. Four projects are selected; two descriptions are drafted.

**Q: What is planned, and what has actually progressed?**

A: The plan is to select six projects and write short descriptions. Four selections and two drafts are complete.

**Meet / overlap:** The selected projects directly serve the portfolio objective, and the completed drafts are evidence of progress.

**Gap:** Two selections and four descriptions remain; no blocker is known.

**Next move:** Draft one description by Friday, then check whether the pace is sustainable.
